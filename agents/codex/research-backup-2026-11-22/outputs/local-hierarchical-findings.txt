Local ring structure plus hierarchical bridges: exploratory findings
2026-11-21

Question
Can local ring-like connectivity and longer-range hierarchical connectivity
combine to sustain exponents near alpha=1.5, beta=0.2?

Main finding
There is a precise mechanism and partial numerical support. Modular local rings
coupled through a hierarchy separate activity into local and collective mode
families. Pooling transfers variance between them. This can extend the range
over which both exponents are near the target. It does not establish a universal
pair, scale invariance, or a biological explanation.

Exact construction and proof
Let N=M*b, with M modules of b neurons. R is a symmetric row-normalized
degree-four ring on b nodes. H is a symmetric row-normalized dyadic hierarchy
on M modules. P=11^T/b projects onto the constant vector in a module.

 B=(1-g) I_M tensor R + g H tensor P.

For every nonconstant ring eigenvector v, Pv=0. Thus any module-supported
vector e_j tensor v is an eigenvector with eigenvalue (1-g)*r, where Rv=r*v.
These M*(b-1) vectors span the within-module zero-sum subspace.
For H z=h*z and constant unit vector c on a module, Rc=c and Pc=c. Thus
z tensor c has eigenvalue (1-g)+g*h. These M collective vectors complete
an orthonormal basis. This is an exact decomposition, independently checked
against dense eigenvalues to absolute tolerance 1e-12.

In x[t+1]=m*B*x[t]+eta[t], put delta=1-m. Relaxation denominators are
 local:      delta + m*g + m*(1-g)*(1-r)
 collective: delta + m*g*(1-h).
Therefore one global coupling can coexist with distinct local and collective
relaxation bands. Adding bridges splits the degenerate uniform modes of the
uncoupled modules; it does not unconditionally slow every mode.

Numerical exploration
309 network specifications were screened at N=256 over 801 coupling values.
Families included rings, weighted hierarchies, their mixtures, module rings,
uniform/random bridges, distance power laws, and stochastic hierarchical
bridges with and without symmetric row balancing. Selected models were tested
with greedy correlation clustering at N=256 and 512. Further selected models
were tested at N=1024 while holding coupling fixed.
An additional 126 module-ring specifications were screened over coupling
and finite autocorrelation horizons. These are exploratory parameter searches,
not independent confirmations or statistical significance tests.

Example with held coupling
Module size b=32, bridge fraction g=0.03, hierarchy Phi=8,
m approximately 0.972837. Horizon: 24 model updates, trapezoidal integral.
Estimator: equal average of each cluster's normalized autocorrelation.
Clusters: greedy correlation pairing. No coupling refit between sizes.

N       global alpha   global beta
256     1.526974       0.239842
512     1.525220       0.239847
1024    1.524599       0.239842

Global fits use dyadic cluster sizes K=2 through 64. For intervals K=8->16,
16->32 and 32->64, alpha is approximately 1.4756--1.5072 and beta approximately
0.1668--0.2254 across these sizes. Outside this window the slopes drift clearly.
Size stability here holds module size and observation window fixed; it does
not show invariance when those scales increase together.

Estimator sensitivity
Finite integration lowers beta without changing the stationary variance alpha.
Related candidates have infinite-horizon beta near 0.30, falling toward the
empirical range at finite horizons. Uniform bridges and pure hierarchies can
also benefit from finite integration, so this effect is not unique to modular
ring connectivity. No mapping from model updates to biological seconds has
been established. The 24-update choice was selected during exploration.

Interpretation
The useful finding is a testable composition mechanism: local modes dominate
small-scale fluctuations, and hierarchical collective modes take over during
pooling. A mixture can sustain near-target exponents better over selected
windows than either component at the same coupling. The exponents remain
functions of topology, coupling, clustering, window, and timescale estimator.

Limitations
These are explicit reconstructed models, not an audit of the unavailable
canonical linear_theory.py or a fit to raw brain data. The strongest analytic
result concerns a weighted deterministic hierarchy. Stochastic hierarchy
controls were explored but do not inherit the exact tensor decomposition.
The candidate was selected after extensive searching, has a fixed module scale,
and misses the exact global pair. No Griffiths phase or universal spectral-
dimension criterion follows from these calculations.
