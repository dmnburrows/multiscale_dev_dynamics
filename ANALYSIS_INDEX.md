## Headline results

- **Mechanism**: α measures mode concentration; β needs several modes sharing variance; criticality destroys mode-sharing (N_eff falls 1000 → 1). Hence an interior, subcritical optimum.
- **What is invariant at α=1.5**: p, the variance share in modes wider than the cluster, is 0.50–0.71 everywhere while 1−m spans 70×.
- **What decides matching**: β_max is set by spectral dimension; β≈0.2 needs d_s≈1. Measured d_s: ring 1.05, torus 2.16, HM 4.08, modular 5.70, ER 12.4.
- **~~Ring and HM build beta by OPPOSITE internal routes~~ SUPERSEDED, see the RETRACTED bullet below.** The dyadic band profile is still a correct description (ring: alpha_b 1.000, 1.174, 1.315, 1.468, 1.677 with beta_b 0.000, 0.000, -0.003, 0.017, 0.034; HM: alpha_b ~2.0-2.25 flat with beta_b 0.10-0.35), but the inference 'two mechanisms' does not follow, because the within/between split is not binning-invariant and the ring's beta_b ~ 0 is forced by circulant symmetry.
- **'The Munn pair is a composition of local and long-range structure' is exactly expressible ONLY for the ring.** Two senses must be kept apart. Weak: the band composition law is exact (6e-15) but holds for any partition of pairs whatsoever, so it says only that a sum can be regrouped -- no content. Strong: range is the canonical coordinate, S_ij = s(d_ij), so the range decomposition is lossless and V(K) = sum_d n_d(K) s(d) is the complete model. eta^2(S|d) = 1.0000 for the ring and <= 0.045 for every other family tested, including the explicitly spatial geometric ones. So the hypothesis is well-posed for the ring and a poor description elsewhere.
- **KEY LIMITATION, not yet resolved**: eta^2 = 1 is exact translation invariance, which no physical system has, so the ring is a singular point and the model set offers NO calibration for what eta^2 a weakly disordered lattice gives, or whether the closed form degrades gracefully. Until a ring-plus-tunable-disorder sweep tracks eta^2, closed-form error and (alpha, beta) together, there is no defensible reading of eta^2 on real data. Expectation (NOT a result): real neural correlations are heterogeneous at fixed separation, so eta^2 will land nearer 0.05 than 1.
- **TERMINOLOGY: do NOT write 'attractor'.** A maximum of beta with respect to m is a STATIONARY POINT (zero derivative), not a dynamical attractor: nothing in this model drives a network toward it, and m is a parameter scanned by hand. A hilltop is stationary and maximally unstable. The defensible claim is about identifiability/parsimony -- if the beta ceiling is ~0.2 then observing beta ~ 0.2 is consistent with a broad range of m and needs no tuning coincidence; if the ceiling is 0.6 then observing 0.2 pins m narrowly and demands an explanation. Claiming an attractor would need a mechanism (e.g. homeostasis adjusting m) and there is none. Also note 'stationary' here collides with 'stationary process' used throughout the code (`stationary_covariances`) -- disambiguate in prose.
- **THE OPERATIVE QUANTITY IS THE BETA CEILING, measured directly by sweeping m; no dimension estimate needed.** Every family has a ceiling with a plateau 0.30-0.83 decades wide. What decides whether the Munn pair is reached without tuning is whether the CEILING ITSELF is ~0.2: ring k=4 0.2212, sw p=0.004 0.2285 (match sits ON the plateau, survives moving 1-m by ~5x) versus ring k=8 0.2815, geo sigma=4 0.2809, hm Phi=3.4 0.4023, er 0.6095 (match sits on a flank, must be aimed). Even ring k=4's plateau is only 0.70 decades, so say 'no FINE tuning', never 'no tuning'.
- **TWO BREAKS in the earlier two-requirements formulation, both now fixed.** (1) Low hop-distance graph dimension is NOT sufficient: ring k=4/k=6/k=8 have graph d 0.93/0.95/0.96 (indistinguishable) with residuals 0.028/0.078/0.121, a 4x spread at constant d. The +0.790 correlation is a BETWEEN-family effect; within rings graph d has zero explanatory power because N(r) ~ kr stays linear in r and cannot see the coupling range k. (2) 'Ordered => flat maximum' is wrong as stated: geo sigma=4's plateau is 0.70 decades, EQUAL to ring k=4's. Its slope-at-best-fit of 0.188 reflects that its Munn match is not ON its plateau (ceiling 0.281), not that it lacks one. Likewise ring k=8 is ordered with a 0.50-decade plateau and still misses -- stationary at the WRONG VALUE.
- **OPEN: ring k=8 is unexplained.** Its beta ceiling of 0.2815 inverts through the continuum beta_max(d) to d ~ 2.6, while both its graph d (0.96) and spectral d_s (1.08) say d ~ 1. `limits_and_dimension.py` flags the likely cause in its own docstring: beta_max(d) assumes a quadratic spectral edge and extended modes, which a k=8 ring may violate. Ring k=4's ceiling 0.221 inverts to d ~ 1.1 against measured d_s = 1.09, so the relation works there.
- **~~TWO INDEPENDENT REQUIREMENTS~~ PARTLY SUPERSEDED by the three bullets above.** (1) **Low graph dimension** decides whether a network can REACH (1.5, 0.2) at all: Spearman +0.790 with the residual after m is optimised away, while best 1-m correlates -0.199. (2) **Translation-invariant order** decides whether that point is STATIONARY rather than tuned: ring k=4 slope 0.038 / window 0.17 dec / 97% of own beta_max, sw p=0.004 slope 0.029 / 0.13 / 97%, versus geo sigma=4 slope 0.188 / 0.11 / 68% and hm Phi=3.4 slope 0.253 / 0.04 / 48%. The 2x2 separates cleanly: ring and sw have both; geo sigma=4 has low graph d (0.99) but is NOT stationary; hm Phi=3.4 reaches the pair (dist 0.044, graph d 1.50) but is NOT stationary; hm Phi=3.0 and er have neither. **Low dimension makes the pair reachable; order makes it an attractor.** Requirement (2) is the same order/disorder axis as `ring_vs_disorder.py` (+0.80 39/39 vs -0.87 0/21), reappearing in a partition-free measurement.
- **SUPERSEDED phrasing to avoid**: 'low effective dimension is what makes the Munn pair an attractor rather than a fit' conflates the two requirements and is refuted by geo sigma=4, which is low-dimensional (graph d 0.99, residual 0.036) and still a fit (68% of ceiling, slope 0.188). Also avoid 'HM is the high-dimensional control': Phi tunes HM's graph dimension (2.26 -> 1.50 over Phi = 2.6 -> 3.4) and Phi=3.4 reaches the pair. HM's real deficit is stationarity, not reach.
- **THE RING'S EXPONENTS DO NOT DEPEND ON CORRELATIONS AT ALL; HM'S DO. New, binning-free, partition-free.** Swap ICG's correlation pairing for wiring-based pairing (`structural_icg.py`) and the ring's entire (alpha, beta) vs m curve is unchanged to 1e-15 even though the two cascades share no pairs; HM's curve moves, its beta ceiling falls 25-35%, and the ceiling is the quantity that `beta_ceiling.py` identified as deciding whether the Munn pair is reached without fine tuning. So for the ring, (alpha, beta) is a property of the NETWORK; for HM it is a property of the network AND the measurement rule. **Caveat, stated plainly: structural pairing does not kill HM's match, it improves it at Phi=3.0 and 2.6** (residual 0.126 -> 0.062 and 0.230 -> 0.144) and worsens it at Phi=3.4. The claim is rule-INVARIANCE, not that HM fails. Second caveat: a random matching beats every structural rule for hm Phi=2.6, so 'the clustering is meaningful' is not what HM's match needs. Third: HM's beta fits are the worst-conditioned in the set (beta R^2 0.83-0.96 vs the ring's 0.98-0.99).
- **THE RING SITS ON AN EXTREMUM; HM DOES NOT. This is the cleanest ring/HM distinction and it needs no partition or binning.** `stationarity_test.py`: the ring's beta at best fit is 97% of its own curve maximum and |dbeta/dlog10(1-m)| = 0.038, 5-10x smaller than any other family, with a 0.17-decade tolerant window. HM Phi=3.0 has beta_max = 0.475 (more than double Munn's 0.2) so it must sit at 48% of its ceiling on a steep flank: slope 0.305, window 0.04 decades. HM's mean best distance (0.112) exceeding its mean window (0.04) shows its match is also SEED-DEPENDENT, while the ring's structure is deterministic. Ring and HM are therefore NOT two equally good explanations of Munn: one is structural, the other is a fit.
- **CORRECTION to the spectral-dimension reading.** beta_max RISES with effective dimension (0.206 at d=0.5 to 0.466 at d=4), so every d can reach beta=0.2; low d is NOT required for that. What low d gives is an INTERIOR maximum of beta at which alpha is pinned to 1.507-1.614 (d = 0.5-2.5); for d >~ 3 the maximum sits at x=0 and there is no interior extremum near alpha=1.5 to land on. So low d_s is required to reach beta=0.2 AND alpha=1.5 SIMULTANEOUSLY and robustly. This resolves the apparent tension between HM's measured d_s = 4.08 and its empirical (1.527, 0.194): HM gets there off-peak, by tuning. Note ring k=8 also fails (dist 0.121), so the requirement is d_s ~ 1, not 'being a ring'.
- **UNVERIFIED LEAD worth chasing**: the negative result below says Munn's cross-species agreement (+-0.02 over 3 decades in N) 'would need each species tuned to +-30%'. The ring's tolerant window is 0.17 decades = +-48% in 1-m. If those two numbers are commensurable, the ring's stationarity is a partial answer to the cross-species puzzle. Check how the +-30% was derived before claiming it.
- **RETRACTED: the ring/HM 'two mechanisms for beta' result.** It rested on splitting beta into within-band and between-band parts, and `band_binning_invariance.py` shows that split is an artifact of bin width: it roughly doubles under refinement for every non-circulant family and exceeds 1 for hm 3.0. Where it is stable (the ring) it is stable because it is a tautology -- S and L are exactly circulant (defect 2e-14), so a single-distance band's tau_b is the ratio of two fixed numbers (tau_1(K) = L_01/S_01 to 4.7e-15 at every K) and beta_b = 0 is arithmetic. Do NOT present ring beta_b ~ 0 as a finding, and do NOT present the band profile as a second mechanism. The band tables remain valid DESCRIPTION of where variance and timescale sit; they are not evidence.
- **What survives of the overnight band work**: (a) the composition law over ranges is exact to 6e-15 but holds for ANY partition of pairs, so it is bookkeeping, not mechanism; (b) two hard binning-free structural facts -- ring ICG clusters contiguous (compactness 1.000+-0.000) vs HM scattered (module purity ~0.50), and ring S exactly circulant vs HM defect 0.98; (c) the ring's closed form, which is the real-space dual of PART 3's Dirichlet form and therefore not new. The only discriminating observable remains the local/global sign test in `ring_vs_disorder.py`, which uses a single fixed two-way split and so has no bin-width freedom.
- **Replacing HM's local structure with an ordered ring causally moves it toward the ring signature.** `range_composition.py hybrid`: as l_min rises 0→6, α_glob−α_loc goes −0.090 → +0.028 → +0.111 → +0.481 (Φ=3.0) and +0.222 → +0.586 (Φ=2.6), monotone at every Φ. But the hybrid does **not** reach (1.5, 0.2) better than pure HM, so composition drives the *mechanism*, not the exponents.
- **Eigenvector delocalisation predicts the local/global signature** (unlike the exponents, which it does not predict — ρ=0.11, p=0.53). Family means are perfectly ordered: ring+shortcuts PR=0.439/diff=+0.852, HM Φ=2.6 PR=0.214/+0.332, HM Φ=3.0 PR=0.113/+0.204, geometric+shortcuts PR=0.047/−1.841; pooled corr=+0.559 over 23 runs.
- **Ring and HM differ STRUCTURALLY (this part stands, it is binning-free).** The ring's ICG clusters are contiguous (compactness 1.000+-0.000), so a cluster of K units holds pairs only out to distance K and each doubling populates one further dyadic distance band from exactly zero. HM's clusters are scattered (module purity ~0.50), so all 10 bands are populated by K=2 and none is populated later. That is a fact about the clusterings. What does NOT follow is any claim about where beta 'comes from' -- see the RETRACTED bullet. Note also that geometric sigma=4 populates new bands at the same rate as the ring but they carry 0.009, 0.000, 0.000 of the variance, so band population counts alone do not separate the families either.
- **Testable consequence (narrowed)**: whether real ICG clusters are spatially contiguous is directly checkable in Munn's zebrafish data, which carries unit positions, and it decides whether the ring's closed form applies at all. It does NOT decide 'which mechanism generates beta', since that question is not well posed. Note the bands here are 1-D ring distance; a 3-D recording needs the banding redefined (pair count at distance d grows like d^2) and that has not been done.
- **"Long-range pairs pool more coherently" requires ORDERED structure, not locality.** α_glob−α_loc, each measured in that family's own metric: ordered ring **+0.80±0.26 (39/39)**, hierarchical modular **+0.27±0.21 (42/48)**, disordered geometric **−0.87±0.74 (0/21)**. Order can be translational (ring) or hierarchical (HM blocks); only *disorder* reverses the sign. The ring's circulant structure gives exact delocalised Fourier modes, so a long-range edge bridges two coherent extended modes; degree disorder localises the eigenvectors, so it bridges two localised patches and the long-range component barely grows (α_glob≈0.53). This is a **discriminating observable**: all three reach (1.5, 0.2) on the total exponents. Untested next step: whether the magnitude tracks eigenvector delocalisation (participation ratio) across the three.
- **Greedy ICG does not recover hierarchical modules.** HM clusters have compactness 0.20–0.23 at K=64, i.e. at the no-information baseline, because HM at Φ=2.2–2.8 has mean edge length 15–35 against the ring's 1.5. HM is not spatially local in the relevant sense.
- **What does generalise across all five topologies**: α rises monotonically toward criticality; β peaks at intermediate m; and β_glob → 0 as m → 1 wherever measurable. The interior subcritical optimum is topology-independent even though the spatial decomposition is not.
- **Methodological trap**: re-optimising m per parameter value destroys any parameter-dependence, because m then moves 20× across the range and compensates. Fix m for every such study (`local_global.py` fixed-m control).
- **The β peak near α=1.5 is not a theorem.** In the bimodal reduction the mixture spread CV(2^γ) peaks at p=1/3, i.e. α=1.415, not 1.5. The observed coincidence is a property of these spectra; state it as an empirical regularity.
- **Negative result**: no model here is scale-invariant. One correlation length gives one crossover, so exponents drift with the fitting window. Munn's cross-species agreement (±0.02 over 3 orders of magnitude in N) would need each species tuned to ±30%.
- **Caveat**: the architecture that matches parameter-free is a 1-D ring, not a hierarchy.

## Known limitations

- The deterministic hierarchy is the mean-field (weighted) model; the stochastic one that Figs 1–2 simulate fragments above Φ≈3.2 and gives different exponents (Δα up to 0.56 at matched windows). They agree only near Φ=2.
- Closed forms for the torus and hypercube describe *box* coarse-graining; greedy ICG is geometrically frustrated beyond 1-D (by K=16 on a 32×32 torus, 63 of 64 clusters have distinct shapes).
- Bipartite graphs have exactly zero cross-class covariance, so ICG never merges across the bipartition.
- Kurtosis of the pairwise-correlation distribution (Munn's third signature) is computable from Σ at each level but has not been run.
